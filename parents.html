Your baby’s registration starts
with your family’s details.

Already have a NIN or applying for your first ID? Follow the guidance that fits each parent.

Already registered for a national ID

Have your NIN ready.

Prepare the information for each parent who has registered. Check the details against the original records.

  1. Bring the national ID or available NIN details for verification.
  2. Check names and dates of birth. Tell the officer about any spelling differences.
  3. If a card is lost or unavailable, ask what alternative evidence is accepted. Do not guess the NIN.

First-time national ID applicants

No ID yet? Tell the officer.

This means a parent has never enrolled for a national ID—not simply that this is their first child.

  1. Identify which parent needs first-time enrolment when you speak to the registration desk.
  2. Ask which identity and residence records are needed for that parent’s circumstances.
  3. Follow the authorised NIRA enrolment process separately. Preparing a newborn record does not issue a parent’s ID.
Get first-time ID help

If only one parent has an ID, explain that situation to the hospital registration desk. Never use another person’s NIN or make up missing information.

Before your hospital visit

Pack the essentials.
Leave the guesswork.

Use this checklist to prepare, not to determine eligibility. The registration officer will confirm the documents needed for your family.

Bring original records where available. Keep any photographs or copies readable, and check that names are consistent across documents.

Missing something? You can still ask for guidance. A checked box is not proof that a document has been accepted.

Careful sharing.

Prepare images only after the hospital confirms what is required. You do not need to share personal documents on this guidance page.

This is a prototype service.Online submission and government verification are not connected. Do not submit personal documents until your hospital confirms an authorised service.
  1. 01

    Photograph the complete document

    Use good light, keep all edges visible and check that every name and number is readable. Avoid glare, filters or edits that change information.

  2. 02

    Prepare the residence confirmation

    If requested, obtain the signed LC residence confirmation in the format advised by the officer. Check the family’s address and the confirming official’s details.

  3. 03

    Keep the chairman’s image for the final step

    Only obtain it if requested, and with the chairman’s consent for this purpose. A photograph alone cannot confirm residence; the record must be verified with the relevant local authority.

Keep copies private. Do not post IDs, birth records or another person’s photograph in public messages or share them through unverified contacts.

When your situation
is a little different.

Families do not all have the same records. Ask for the right next step rather than filling in uncertain details.

Get registration help
What if a parent is unavailable, or I am a guardian?

Explain your relationship to the child and which parent details are available. The hospital officer can advise on consent, guardianship or other supporting records. Do not enter yourself as a parent unless that is accurate.

What if names or dates do not match?

Show the officer the records with the different details before submission. Ask which record needs correction and which authority can correct it. Do not alter a document image to make it match.

Does birth registration automatically give my baby a NIN?

No. Parent information and supporting records need verification. NIN allocation is subject to NIRA’s authorised process and approval. A hospital birth record is not itself a NIN, national ID or birth certificate.

What should I keep after submitting?

When using an authorised service, keep any acknowledgement and application reference you receive. Ask how you will be notified of corrections or next steps. You can visit Track an application; live status updates are not connected in this prototype.
